# One bounded EFS service handoff after the early ruleset reply

The November 25 MODEM EFS EARLY ERROR photos show the first EFS RRQ accepted at `00E0D345`, the earlier ruleset dispatch completed at `00E1AB1F`, and the EFS dispatch completed at `00EF0F64`. EFS was already held before ruleset dispatch completed, yet waited approximately 933 ms in total before its own dispatch completed. The photo does not divide that interval into individual validations, router callbacks and other runtime services.

The selected runtime normally calls its TFTP handler once per service pass. On ResponseQueued it performs the existing bounded router and close handling, then proceeds through registry, storage, IPA and listener work before another outer router/service pass. The actual Owner replay accepts ruleset and EFS requests together: the first ordinary service call handles the ruleset and leaves EFS held. One further ordinary call can dispatch EFS without waiting for that next full pass. This establishes a service opportunity, not a reconstruction of handset elapsed costs or a guarantee of the modem's private deadline.

## Scope and boundaries

`sunfish-efs-service-handoff` depends on the existing first-EFS checked writer experiment. It is default-off in the Owner and explicitly enabled by the native runtime. At validated receive intake it retains the first exact ruleset RRQ's actual opaque handle. The existing early-EFS scope retains the first exact EFS RRQ's handle and peer. Successful normal queue admission of that ruleset reply latches the prerequisite; no timestamp, diagnostic counter or observed reply kind authorizes the handoff.

After the existing normal TFTP handler and its router/close paths, the runtime may invoke exactly one additional ordinary service step for the boot. It requires that the original EFS handle is currently first in the held array, its peer has no known close, no retained TFTP reply is waiting, and no MCFG or firmware close handoff is pending. Older held requests cannot be overtaken. An earlier ineligible check does not spend the opportunity. Once eligible, the latch is spent before fallible dispatch or I/O. The handle cannot rebind to a later EFS request.

Native uses the full existing combined TFTP server with its real firmware provider, close resolution and peer snapshot. The extra step uses ordinary server policy, checked request access, retained reply creation, request-slot release, queue admission and continuation scheduling. Response/ignored counters include its result. It adds no router loop or direct transmit selection. The inherited EFS writer group can run after successful admission, and retains its fresh-RX veto, advertisement-before-ACK, normal QRTR credits, fitting intents and blocked-write fallback. Published buffers still require real matching receipts. An extra service step performs its ordinary validations; no validation boundary is removed or batched across callbacks. Scheduling and the timing/count of normal I/O can change.

The first ruleset reply need only be queued, not completed; it is a different peer in the observed boot. Same-peer pressure still yields normal retained-reply/flow behavior. A known EFS close prevents the borrowed step; pending transport close cleanup remains serialized through the original paths. Errors remain terminal. No firmware bytes, EFS file policy, IRQ/SMC authority, mapping or ownership grant changes.

## Offline evidence and panel

Actual Owner cases cover the accepted ruleset/EFS pair, default-off and absent prerequisite, an older held request, known close, terminal readiness fault, a previously published same-peer flow surviving until its real completion, and the actual combined firmware-provider branch. Both exact responses complete against their own real receipts. In the paired-versus-ordinary synthetic fixture, the full GLINK wire and ordered QRTR packets are identical. The experiment changes when the second normal service call runs, not those reply bytes. Expected failing controls remove either the ruleset prerequisite or exact head-handle guard. Full owner, IRQ, Core, grant/credit, close, backpressure and fault regressions remain required.

**EFS SERVICE HANDOFF** retains the original final ACK, ELF and first-two-per-path EFS timelines and FIFO receipt bounds. Row16 becomes `VISIBLE CLOSE FIN EVICT STOP CMDS`; the fifth value packs up to four existing early-writer command IDs in successive low-to-high bytes. Use the write count to identify valid entries; unused entries are zero and other operations project as 0xff. Known commands are 6 RX_DONE, 5 intent advertisement, 8 grant ACK and 9 DATA. No new device read or clock hook supplies that projection.

Row17 is `EARLY CALL WRITE RX BLOCK SVC STATE`. Its first four values retain the writer-group counts. SVC counts the extra service attempt (0 or 1). STATE is 0 no attempt, 1 ordinary Idle, 2 ResponseQueued, 3 WaitingReply, 4 Closed, 5 Ignored or 6 error. An SVC attempt is not proof that its packet published: use the exact first-EFS queue/publication/peer-tail/receipt clocks. The inherited writer STOP meanings are unchanged.

The next cold boot tests whether SVC runs and reduces EFS acceptance-to-dispatch/publication, whether the modem still consumes it promptly, and whether completion or WLAN/WLFW changes. It also distinguishes the earlier writer's committed control command. It does not identify an unrecorded incoming header, establish a proprietary application timeout or promise WLAN startup. Prior Linux/Android audits still show missing-file ERROR responses compatible with the successful reference and later MCFG/digest occurring after WLAN; host source does not expose the modem's complete private launch decision.
